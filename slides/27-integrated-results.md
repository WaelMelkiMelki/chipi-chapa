Note:
This slide zooms out. Seven stages, one narrative. Twelve thousand four hundred and twelve raw subscribers enter the pipeline. Nine thousand seven hundred and eighty-three are excluded — 78.8 percent, of which 98.9 percent are RF-driven. Two thousand six hundred and twenty-nine pass the eligibility gate — 21.2 percent of the base. Within the eligible population, 1,181 are in the Premium suitability band and 1,448 are in Standard. Nine hundred and thirteen receive a value-add bundle recommendation — 34.7 percent. Six hundred and fifty-seven are flagged as at-risk for retention. And six hundred and fifty-eight form the Wave 1 Contact Now pilot list. Four findings summarise the analysis. First, coverage is the binding constraint, not capacity: 98.9 percent of exclusions are RF-driven; zero subscribers are blocked by the PRB gate. Second, the addressable market is 2,629 subscribers — 21.2 percent of the base — with 5G-feasibility at only 2.3 percent. Third, 34.7 percent of the eligible population receives a value-added offer, meaning the pipeline is not only a filter but a monetisation framework. And fourth, the top decile is dominated by Cluster 0 and Cluster 2 — the MCDA isolates high-value segments from the mass-market base. Now, interpretation guidelines — the honest framing. Priority scores are a relative ranking, not an absolute probability of success. Churn probabilities are calibrated on an imbalanced test set and should be used as a risk ordering, not a guaranteed prediction. The XGBoost regressor's high R-squared is expected because it re-encodes the manual MCDA formula — it is not a new predictive pattern. And the capacity simulation is a proxy: "one hundred percent approved" means safe under the adopted assumptions, not guaranteed safe in a live network. What the pipeline ultimately produces is a decision-support framework for coverage-qualified, capacity-safe, service-personalised FWA migration — ready for business review and pilot execution.
