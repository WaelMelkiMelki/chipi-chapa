Note:
In summary: this project addressed an engineering decision problem at the intersection of 5G radio planning, customer analytics, and commercial strategy. The objective was to identify which existing mobile subscribers can be migrated to Fixed Wireless Access, which personalised offer each candidate should receive, and whether the radio access network can absorb that migration without cannibalising fibre or overloading the network. What was delivered is a five-layer analytics pipeline: from raw data reliability and feature engineering, through a unified subscriber-month Analytical Base Table, to four decision layers — eligibility scoring, K-Prototypes segmentation, MCDA prioritisation, and XGBoost churn risk. What this means is that the FWA opportunity is coverage-constrained, not capacity-constrained: ninety-eight point nine percent of exclusions are RF-driven, and zero subscribers are blocked by the PRB capacity gate. The addressable market is two thousand six hundred and twenty-nine subscribers, refined to six hundred and fifty-eight Wave 1 Contact Now candidates. And the framework is not only a filter — it is also a monetisation engine: thirty-four point seven percent of eligible subscribers receive a value-add bundle. Three honest boundaries must be stated. The capacity simulation is a PRB proxy, not a full MAC-layer scheduler simulation. There are no historical campaign-response labels available for supervised propensity modelling. And the results depend on the accuracy and completeness of the address-level coverage grid. The forward path is threefold: multi-month validation to capture seasonal variation, supervised conversion-rate modelling once pilot data is available, and integration of the recommendation outputs into operational CRM workflows with a feedback loop. What this project ultimately provides is a decision-support framework for targeted, coverage-qualified, capacity-safe, service-personalised 5G FWA migration — ready for pilot execution.
